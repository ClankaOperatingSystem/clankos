review: how a garden is reviewed

"clankos-run help review" prints this document from the ClankOS image
the garden runs. A review is not a command: it is read and decided by
the person, with the agent preparing what is to be read. The commands
named here have protocols of their own.


1. What a review reads, and how it is marked

No file name carries meaning. The commands read every Org file of a
scope, and what a heading is for is told by its tag, its keyword and
its dates. The one exception is intray.org, the file pos-capture
writes to.

    :review:    a scheduled, repeating item: the occasion on which the
                scope it lies in is reviewed. Marking it done
                schedules the next.
    :purpose:   what the scope is for, and the standard it holds. A
                responsibility's purpose is read at its review and at
                the root's.
    :goal:      something the scope is working towards. Two kinds, told
                by the keyword: a heading with a TODO keyword is a goal
                that can be reached, and is marked DONE when it is,
                with a DEADLINE if it has a date; a heading with no
                keyword is a goal that is held, never done, which the
                reachable ones serve.

A goal links down to the projects and repeating tasks that advance it.
Nothing links up. A review finds what the links leave out: a goal with
no live project under it, an active project no goal names.

A project is told by its file's STATUS (docs/responsibility-tree.txt)
and needs no tag. A task is told by its keyword. A repeating task with
a LOGBOOK is a habit; a :habit: tag may come later, so that a review
can report how often it is kept.


2. Three reviews, told by scope

A review item is an open heading tagged :review: with a SCHEDULED
date, in a file of the scope it reviews. What the review is of is the
kind of that scope. No tag or property names a kind of review.

    project          one project: its own file holds the item. Still
                     wanted; outcome still right; next action true;
                     review date right.
    responsibility   an area and the projects under it: a file of the
                     responsibility holds the item. Purpose still
                     held; goals still wanted; the right projects
                     running; nothing left dormant.
    the whole        the root: the person, the practice and the tree.
                     Two items, a weekly one and a four-weekly one
                     (sections 5 and 6), in a file of the root.

A container's review includes its children's: a responsibility's
review looks over its projects, and the root's over everything.

Only the root reviews the whole. A repository grafted into a garden is
a responsibility of that garden, and its reviews are a
responsibility's.

A review item may be kept in the scope above the one it reviews, as
when the scope is a repository other people share and the review is
one person's. The item then names the scope in a COVERS property, by
its path from the directory of the item's file:

    * TODO Review the shop :review:
    SCHEDULED: <2030-03-04 Mon +1w>
    :PROPERTIES:
    :COVERS:   shop
    :END:

It is still listed among the reviews of the scope whose file holds
it. An item covers its own scope and those it names, and no other.

startup-prompt lists review items late or due in the next 7 days,
projects', responsibilities' and the root's apart, and names each
active project, responsibility and root that no review covers. A
new garden has none: the person writes the root's two items, in
whichever file of the root they choose, as section 7 shows.


3. Doing any review

  - Read the scope: its Org files, and what startup-prompt lists for
    it.
  - Decide, and edit the files: keywords, dates, outcomes, next
    actions, goals. Capture what comes to mind with pos-capture rather
    than holding it.
  - Write what was found where it will be read again: in the project
    file, or beside the scope's purpose and goals. A note on the
    review item itself goes into its LOGBOOK when it is marked done.
  - Mark the item DONE. Its repeater moves the date forward; the next
    review is scheduled by doing this one.
  - A finding about ClankOS itself, a command that got in the way or
    could do more, goes to an issue (section 8).


4. A project or a responsibility

A project's review asks whether the outcome is still wanted and still
right; whether the next action is true, the one thing that would move
it; whether the project is blocked, and on whom; which goal it
advances; and whether its review interval fits its pace. A project
that has reached its outcome, or is no longer wanted, is closed: its
outcome recorded, and the file's STATUS set to COMPLETE, or the file
retired.

A responsibility's review asks whether its purpose is still the one
held; whether each held goal is still wanted and each reachable goal
is advancing, or reached; whether the projects under it are the right
ones, and whether any has gone quiet without being closed; whether a
goal has no project advancing it, or a project advances no goal; and
whether its own interval fits. A responsibility may hold a review on
someone else's cadence as well as its own, such as an employer's
quarterly conversation, and each is its own item. Its projects'
reviews are not repeated here; they have their own items.


5. The weekly review

Fast and shallow: it keeps the inventory one that can be trusted. It
is GTD's weekly review, in this garden's terms.

    Get clear      Capture what is in the head and on loose paper with
                   pos-capture. Empty the intray with pos-refile, each
                   item placed or dropped. Retire the week's done items
                   with pos-sweep.
    Get current    Read "startup-prompt --weekly". Every NEXT item
                   is still the next thing. Every WAITING item is
                   still waited for. Scheduled items and deadlines
                   are right. Every late review is done or
                   rescheduled. Every project named as having no
                   next action gets one. Every active project and
                   responsibility named as having no review gets
                   one. Look back over what was finished, and over
                   the week's calendar for what it left behind,
                   and forward for what is coming.
    Get creative   Anything wanted but not committed to is written
                   down, in the intray or where it belongs.

Then mark the weekly item done. The daily review is reading
startup-prompt; it needs no item.


6. The four-weekly review

Slower and deeper. It falls on the same weekday as the weekly review,
once every four weeks, and is done in the same sitting after it. The
weekly pass asks whether the system is in order; this pass asks
whether the practice helps.

  - Each responsibility's purpose and goals, in turn: is the purpose
    still the right one, is each goal still wanted or advancing, and
    is the tree of responsibilities still the shape of the life?
  - The root's own goals, against what the projects are actually
    doing; and the root's purpose, whether what is being done serves
    it. Write here what is found.
  - What has gone quiet: a scope whose swept done items have thinned
    over the four weeks, and was not meant to rest.
  - The practice itself, over the four weeks:
        attention    was anything held in the head that should have
                     been captured, or captured and lost?
        trust        did what mattered surface in time, and did
                     deferred things come back?
        clarity      could work begin from a reminder without
                     reconstructing it?
        choice       did circumstances and purpose shape what was
                     chosen, or only what was visible?
        proportion   did the system cost more than it gave back?
    An answer that points at ClankOS goes to an issue (section 8); one
    that points at the practice goes into how the person works.

Then mark the four-weekly item done.


7. Writing and tuning the items

The root's two items, in any Org file of the root, on the same
weekday:

    * Reviews

    ** TODO Weekly review                                     :review:
    SCHEDULED: <2026-10-12 Mon ++1w>

    ** TODO Monthly review                                    :review:
    SCHEDULED: <2026-11-02 Mon ++4w>

The four-weekly one is first due on the fourth weekly date, so that
the first deep pass follows three shallow ones. A responsibility's
item is the same, in a file of the responsibility, at its own
interval.

The person tunes a review by editing its item: the interval, the
weekday, or removing the item. No configuration holds an interval.

Each uses the ++ repeater: a review done late is next due on its
cadence, not a week from the day it was done ".+1w", and not left in
the past "+1w". A longer horizon, quarterly or yearly, is one more
item with a longer interval.


8. Findings about ClankOS

Any review may find that ClankOS got in the way or could do more. Such
a finding is an issue on the repository concerned, at
https://github.com/ClankaOperatingSystem: poslib for the Emacs
commands and the formats, pyposlib for the Python library, clankos for
the image, its scripts and these documents. A finding about the
practice rather than the commands may also be raised there, as a
question for the methodology pages.
