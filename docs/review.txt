review: how a garden is reviewed

"clankos-run help review" prints this document from the ClankOS image
the garden runs. A review is not a command: it is read and decided by
the person, with the agent preparing what is to be read. The commands
named here have protocols of their own.


1. Three reviews, told by scope

A review item is an open heading tagged :review: with a SCHEDULED
date, in a file of the scope it reviews. What the review is of is the
kind of that scope. No tag or property names a kind of review.

    project          one project: its own file holds the item. Still
                     wanted; outcome still right; next action true;
                     review date right.
    responsibility   an area and the projects under it: its Org
                     entrance holds the item. Standard still met; the
                     right projects running; nothing left dormant.
    the whole        the root: the person, the practice and the tree.
                     life.org holds two items, a weekly one and a
                     four-weekly one (sections 4 and 5).

A container's review includes its children's: a responsibility's
review looks over its projects, and the root's over everything.

Only the root reviews the whole. A repository grafted into a garden is
a responsibility of that garden, and has no life.org
(docs/initiate.txt).

startup-prompt lists review items late or due in the next 7 days,
projects', responsibilities' and the root's apart, and names each
active project, responsibility and root that has none scheduled.


2. Doing any review

  - Read the scope: its Org files, and what startup-prompt lists for
    it.
  - Decide, and edit the files: keywords, dates, outcomes, next
    actions. Capture what comes to mind with pos-capture rather than
    holding it.
  - Write what was found where it will be read again: in the project
    file, the responsibility's entrance, or life.org. A note on the
    review item itself goes into its LOGBOOK when it is marked done.
  - Mark the item DONE. Its repeater moves the date forward; the next
    review is scheduled by doing this one.
  - A finding about ClankOS itself, a command that got in the way or
    could do more, goes to an issue (section 7).


3. A project or a responsibility

A project's review asks whether the outcome is still wanted and still
right; whether the next action is true, the one thing that would move
it; whether the project is blocked, and on whom; and whether its review
interval fits its pace. A project that has reached its outcome, or is
no longer wanted, is closed: its outcome recorded, and the file's
STATUS set to COMPLETE, or the file retired.

A responsibility's review asks whether the standard it holds is still
being met; whether the projects under it are the right ones, and
whether any has gone quiet without being closed; whether something it
should hold has no project; and whether its own interval fits. Its
projects' reviews are not repeated here; they have their own items.


4. The weekly review

Fast and shallow: it keeps the inventory one that can be trusted. It
is GTD's weekly review, in this garden's terms.

    Get clear      Capture what is in the head and on loose paper with
                   pos-capture. Empty the intray with pos-refile, each
                   item placed or dropped. Retire the week's done items
                   with pos-sweep.
    Get current    Read startup-prompt. Every NEXT item is still the
                   next thing. Scheduled items and deadlines are right.
                   Every late review is done or rescheduled. Every
                   active project and responsibility named as having
                   no review gets one. Look back over the week's
                   calendar for what it left behind, and forward for
                   what is coming.
    Get creative   Anything wanted but not committed to is written
                   down, in the intray or where it belongs.

Then mark the weekly item done.


5. The four-weekly review

Slower and deeper. It falls on the same weekday as the weekly review,
once every four weeks, and is done in the same sitting after it. The
weekly pass asks whether the system is in order; this pass asks
whether the practice helps.

  - Each responsibility's entrance, in turn: is the standard still
    the right one, and is the tree of responsibilities still the shape
    of the life?
  - life.org: Goals, for the next year or two, against what the
    projects are actually doing; Vision, further out; Purpose and
    principles, whether what is being done serves them. Write here
    what is found; the headings are empty until the person fills them.
  - The practice itself, over the four weeks:
        attention    was anything held in the head that should have
                     been captured, or captured and lost?
        trust        did what mattered surface in time, and did
                     deferred things come back?
        clarity      could work begin from a reminder without
                     reconstructing it?
        choice       did circumstances and purpose shape what was
                     chosen, or only what was visible?
        proportion   did the system cost more than it gave back?
    An answer that points at ClankOS goes to an issue (section 7); one
    that points at the practice goes into how the person works.

Then mark the four-weekly item done.


6. Tuning

The person tunes a review by editing its item: the interval, the
weekday, or removing the item. No configuration holds an interval.

Each item written by initiate uses the ++ repeater, "++1w" and
"++4w": a review done late is next due on its cadence, not a week
from the day it was done ".+1w", and not left in the past "+1w". A
longer horizon, quarterly or yearly, is one more item under Reviews in
life.org, with a longer interval.


7. Findings about ClankOS

Any review may find that ClankOS got in the way or could do more. Such
a finding is an issue on the repository concerned, at
https://github.com/ClankaOperatingSystem: poslib for the Emacs
commands and the formats, pyposlib for the Python library, clankos for
the image, its scripts and these documents. A finding about the
practice rather than the commands may also be raised there, as a
question for the methodology pages.
